# The six stages

Attention → Relevance → Retention → Reward → Sharing → Relationship. A post can be excellent on
five stages and still fail — each is a gate, not a score to average. Use this to diagnose *where*
an idea or draft breaks, not to rate it overall.

## Attention

**Requires:** something legible in the first frame or first line that is not the setup. A viewer
decides in seconds whether this is for them.

**Checkable condition:** could the viewer say what this is, what is happening, or why to keep
watching, before any explanation runs? If the answer needs the second sentence, Attention fails.

## Relevance

**Requires:** the viewer recognizes themselves, their situation, or something they care about in
what is being shown — not just that the subject is generic ("concert," "phone camera") but
specific ("the moment you realize your framing ruined the one shot you can't reshoot").

**Checkable condition:** does this speak to a person who has been in this exact situation, or only
to a person who likes the general topic? The first is Relevance; the second is a hope.

## Retention

**Requires:** each beat earns the next one. A viewer keeps watching because something is still
open, not because they haven't yet decided to leave.

**Checkable condition — retention vs. satisfaction:** a viewer can watch to the end and still feel
robbed. Manufactured retention (a promise stretched past its payoff, a cut that implies more than
it delivers) scores well on watch-through and poisons the relationship with the next post. Retain
attention by keeping a real question open, not by delaying its answer past patience.

## Reward

**Requires:** the viewer gets something at the close — an answer, a technique, a feeling landed,
recognition. This is the payoff the Attention stage opened a gap for.

**Four value types.** Name which one (or which two) a piece delivers. A piece combining two is
stronger than a piece delivering one at higher volume:

| Value | What the viewer leaves with |
|---|---|
| Utility | A technique, setting, or fact they can use |
| Entertainment | A feeling — amusement, awe, tension released |
| Identity | Confirmation of who they are or want to be |
| Connection | The sense of shared experience with the creator or a group |

**Checkable condition:** name the value type before publishing. "It's good footage" is not a value
type — it is the absence of one.

## Sharing

**Requires:** a reason to travel through a relationship, not just past a thumb.

**Sharing is identity, not enjoyment.** People forward what says something about them to the
specific person they send it to. A share is a stronger signal than a like because it costs
something — it stakes a small claim about the sender's taste or knowledge.

**Emotional payload taxonomy** — discrete emotions predict sharing; **valence does not, and
neither does arousal** — the arousal theory failed to replicate, and a head-to-head test on 4,000+
annotated posts found the discrete-emotion model won (`references/relatability.md` carries the
evidence grade for each emotion below). Name one dominant driver per piece:

- Amusement
- Recognition ("this is exactly my experience")
- Curiosity
- Awe
- Surprise
- Nostalgia
- Anger / injustice
- Hope
- Sadness — **do not avoid it.** The often-cited finding that sadness suppresses sharing comes
  from 2008 newspaper email; two larger later studies, one randomized, found the opposite

**Checkable condition — the send test:** who forwards this, to whom specifically, and what does
sending it say about the sender? "Someone might like this" is not an answer. "A friend who just
bought their first mirrorless camera, because it says 'I get why you're frustrated'" is.

## Relationship

**Requires:** the viewer's model of the creator gets more specific after this post than before it,
not just reinforced.

**Checkable condition:** does this post teach the audience something true and consistent about who
made it (a real point of view, a recognizable craft habit) or is it interchangeable with content
from any account in the niche? Interchangeable content can perform once and builds nothing.

## Using this file

`ff-ideas` checks a premise against Attention and Reward before development. `ff-critique` checks
a finished draft against Retention, Sharing, and Relationship — the stages a draft can still fail
even when the premise was sound.
