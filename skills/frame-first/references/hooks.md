# Hook architectures

Each architecture below is a shape, not a script. Use one per piece — mixing two in a 15-second
open dilutes both. Check `profile/hooks-used.md` before choosing; repeating the same shape four posts
running trains the audience to scroll before the hook lands.

## Tension between two truths

State two things that both hold and appear to contradict. The viewer stays to see how.

> "This is the best camera I've used for concerts, and I still wouldn't buy it." (Opens on price
> or a dealbreaker most reviews skip — the payoff is naming the specific tradeoff.)

> "I had the best seat in the venue and got my worst footage of the tour." (Payoff: what the seat
> actually cost — sightline, motion, or a lighting angle nobody warns you about.)

## The overlooked detail

Name what everyone photographs, then point at what you noticed instead. Works because it implies
craft without stating it.

> "Everyone films the drop. This is the ten seconds before it that nobody points a phone at."

> "Every video from tonight has the same shot of the stage. Here's the one thing that happened in
> the pit that none of them caught."

## Pattern interruption

Four kinds — pick the one the footage actually supports:

- **Visual** — open on a frame that doesn't match the expected genre (a close-up of a hand, not
  the stage) so the eye stays to resolve it.
- **Verbal** — open mid-thought, as if the viewer walked in partway through a real conversation.
- **Structural** — start at the interesting moment, not the setup. Cut the walk-in, the "so today
  I'm at—", the establishing shot that establishes nothing.
- **Conceptual** — open on a contradiction to the viewer's assumption about the topic itself, not
  just about this video.

## The curiosity gap, and the obligation to close it

Open a specific unresolved question — not vague intrigue, an actual question with an actual
answer coming. Then close it before the piece ends.

> Opens: "The photographer next to me had a $6,000 setup. I got the better shot with this." Pays
> off: names the actual technique or positioning that mattered more than the gear.

**Clickbait is this shape with the close removed.** A gap opened and never paid off is not a
hook — it is the single fastest way to lose the Sharing stage (`references/arrrsr.md`), because a
viewer who feels baited does not forward the post and does not return.

## Story architecture in 15 seconds

Setup → tension → progression → payoff → meaning. Not all five need a spoken line — a hard cut can
carry setup, a facial reaction can carry tension. But check that all five exist *somewhere* in the
edit, even wordlessly. A piece missing "meaning" is technically competent and forgettable.

## Recurring micro-story formats

Templates for framing a premise once it exists — not premises themselves:

- **"The part nobody photographs"** — pairs with the overlooked-detail hook above.
- **"I went looking for X and found Y"** — sets up a genuine pivot; only use it if the pivot is
  real, not a marketing turn.
- **"Things nobody tells you about..."** — needs at least one item that is actually not told
  elsewhere, or it reads as the generic list format `references/slop-patterns.md` flags.

## Instant comprehension

Independent of which architecture is used: within the first moments the viewer should know what
this is, what is happening, or why they should care — at least one of the three, ideally two.
A technically interesting hook that requires context the viewer doesn't have yet fails Attention
(`references/arrrsr.md`) before the architecture gets a chance to work.
