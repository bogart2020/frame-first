# Closings

`references/hooks.md` governs how a piece opens. This file governs how it ends. Same shape: each
closing below is a form, not a script, and one piece uses one.

The opening buys attention; the closing decides what the viewer does with it. A piece that ends
badly is not a piece that ends weakly — it is a piece whose whole middle stops counting, because
the last line is what the viewer carries into the decision to send it, save it, or scroll on.

## End on a physical action

Close on something someone did, or something the creator will do next. It gives the viewer a place
to stand after the piece stops.

> "I stayed until the crew started pulling the cable."

> "Next one I'm shooting from the back."

Works because an action is checkable and finite. The viewer does not have to interpret it, and it
leaves the piece over rather than trailing.

## End on a step forward

Name what changes now — a decision, a next attempt, a thing that will be different. Distinct from a
call to action: nothing is being asked of the viewer.

> "I'm not bringing the zoom next time."

The failure to avoid is the disguised call to action. "What would you do?" is not a step forward,
it is engagement bait (`references/slop-patterns.md`), and Meta names it as demoted
(`references/ranking-signals.md`).

## End on the wound

Close on the unresolved thing and leave it unresolved. The strongest ending available when the
piece is about something that did not resolve in life either.

> "some things you have to accept"

**Two conditions, and both are required.** The wound has to be the actual subject of the piece —
not a sad line bolted onto a craft post. And it must not be a curiosity gap: a gap is a question
the piece promised to answer and owes the viewer (`references/hooks.md`). A wound is an answer the
piece already gave, and the discomfort is the point. Ending on an unpaid gap is clickbait; ending
on a wound is the piece being honest about what happened.

**Do not soften it in a polish pass.** Models default to hopeful and avoid darker material, so the
instinct will be to add a redemptive last line. For a creator whose strongest work sits in a
melancholy register, that instinct removes the human signal — see `references/slop-patterns.md`,
"What human readers actually notice".

## Weight comes from withholding

The line that lands is usually the one that says less than it could. Naming the emotion is what
drains it: the reader who is told what to feel has nothing left to do.

| Stated | Withheld |
|---|---|
| "It was devastating and I'll never forget it." | "I didn't take another photo after that." |
| "I felt so lucky to be there." | "I was three feet from the barrier." |

The right column contains no emotional word at all. The emotion is what the reader supplies, which
is also what makes them feel it rather than read it — the same mechanism as self-reference in the
`ff-critique` gate.

**The limit:** withholding only works when something was actually given. A line that withholds
everything is not restraint, it is a blank, and a blank reads as having nothing to say.

## The failure this file exists to catch

**A last line that restates the first.** Already named in `references/slop-patterns.md` under
structural shapes — if the closing could replace the opening with no loss, nothing was said in
between, and the piece is a loop rather than a shape. Do not re-derive the test here; run that one.

Two more, both narrower:

- **The summary close.** Restating what the viewer just watched. They watched it.
- **The pivot to a lesson nobody asked for.** A craft observation ending on a life aphorism reads
  as reaching, and it is where the abstraction-as-subject failure most often appears
  (`references/slop-patterns.md`).
